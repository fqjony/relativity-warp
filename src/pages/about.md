## What shaped my approach

I started with web applications. My work grew into data integration, cloud infrastructure, delivery automation, security, and reliability. Each layer changed how I understood the others.

Building applications taught me to think about behavior and interfaces. Data integration taught me to connect different representations without losing their meaning. Operations made me pay closer attention to dependencies, failure, ownership, and the consequences of change.

Over time, I became as interested in how engineering work happens as in the systems being built.

## How I work

I like investigating what happens beneath an abstraction: where information comes from, how components interact, and which assumptions hold them together.

I combine hands-on work, code, scripts, CLI tools, documentation, and agents. I prefer small, composable solutions with clear inputs, outputs, responsibilities, and checks. Evidence guides the work; judgment handles the uncertainty and tradeoffs.

Automation means understanding something well enough to make it repeatable while keeping control over what matters.

## Why Relativity Warp

The name is a nod to [Einstein’s work on relativity](https://www.einstein-online.info/en/spotlights/): frames of reference and the curvature of spacetime. It made me think about engineering differently—how the way I understand a system shapes the way I build it, and how changing perspective can reveal another path through the same problem. Here, “warp” is a metaphor for that shift in thinking.

Useful understanding often disappears between tasks, tools, and sessions. A problem gets solved, but the reasoning stays in a conversation, a temporary file, or someone’s head.

This site is where I turn that experience into questions, concepts, models, and dated notes. The [Engineering Memory Model](/models/) explores how a lesson remains checkable, revisable, and useful in later work. [A Lesson Needs a Destination](/spectrum/2026-09-13-a-lesson-needs-a-destination/) asks where that understanding can live and be maintained.

A lesson might become a document, test, configuration, script, or tool. People and agents may need different forms of it; the meaning and evidence should stay connected.

My recurring challenge is capturing what matters before its destination is clear—especially when sensitive work needs prompt cleanup. I want less context to reconstruct, fewer discoveries to repeat, and more useful patterns to carry forward.

## Working together

I value calm investigation, clear decisions, and practical reasoning. Be concise, concrete, and curious. Challenge assumptions, explain tradeoffs, and keep changes reviewable. The best collaboration develops understanding alongside the solution.
